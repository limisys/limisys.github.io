The epistemic ledger

Every claim, its tag, and where its evidence lives
On this pageTag ruleThe ledger

This page is the site's index of what is known and how it is known. It is not a disclaimer — the disclaimers are on the Scope page. This is an inventory: for each statement the theory makes, which of five tags it carries, and where a reader can go to check it.

The rule that generates the tags

The programme holds itself to one discipline, and it is the reason this site is organised the way it is:

A claim may enter only in one of two legal forms — (1) a declaration that compiles in Lean with zero sorry, or (2) a faithful report of a cited source. Everything else is an interpretation, must carry its tag, and may not appear in a theorem position.

Theorem and interpretation are kept strictly apart — the paper calls this the wall of caution. "Theorem" means proved, classically or in Lean, and nothing else.

TagMeansHow a reader checks it
DefinitionA stipulation. Nothing to prove; it fixes vocabulary.Read it; check it is used consistently.
AssumptionA premise the theory does not derive.Ask whether it holds of the system you care about.
Machine-CheckedA theorem with a machine-checked proof.Run the reproduction command.
ConjectureStated, not proved. May be false.Look for a counterexample.
Empirical predictionA claim about the world, untested.Run the experiment; it is designed to be able to fail.

Explicitly not claimed

Not claimed
Any commitment to qualia, phenomenal consciousness, or first-person experience.
Any use of, or commitment to, the free-energy principle.
That any real system has been tested.
That the formal class captures autonomy in general. Whether it does is the empirical question.
How to use this page. If a statement you are reading elsewhere does not appear here, it is not part of the theory's ledger — treat it as commentary. If it appears with a tag, that tag is the theory's own claim about its own strength. Where the tag says Machine-Checked, the reproduction command is the evidence; where it says Conjecture or Empirical, nothing has been proved.
