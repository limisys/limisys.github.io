Scope, and limitations stated against interest

Placed before the mathematics, on purpose

A paper that claims to state its own weaknesses is worth nothing if the caveats arrive after the reader has already formed an impression. So they are here — before the theory pages, not after them.

Three red lines

These run through the whole programme:

No commitment to qualia or first-person experience"Boundary" names an operationalisable attribution-discrimination capacity, not an ontological entity.
No engagement with the free-energy principleThis work does not use it. α is a modelling parameter for the price of an unclear boundary, and carries no variational-free-energy or surprise commitment.
No claim that any real system has been testedAll conclusions are theory, deterministic computation, and formal verification.

What grade of "general" is claimed

"General" is used in two incompatible senses. In ordinary use it means widely applicable. In category theory universal is a technical term: characterised by a universal property, hence unique up to unique isomorphism — which does not imply wide applicability. So the programme states the grade rather than letting "general" do work it cannot do.

Two legal forms of a claim

The programme holds itself to one discipline, and it is the reason this site looks the way it does:

A claim may enter only in one of two legal forms — (1) a declaration that compiles in Lean with zero sorry, or (2) a faithful report of a cited source. Everything else is an interpretation, must carry its tag, and may not appear in a theorem position.

That is why every statement on this site carries one of five labels: Definition Assumption Machine-Checked Conjecture Empirical prediction

And the closing line the programme uses for itself. Every theorem here is proved within the formal framework and states that the model's own defined quantities satisfy their defining relations. None of them may be cited as an empirical statement about real minds, brains, or robots. This is a theoretical foundation, built to be broken by experiment; its fate belongs to the bench, not to the axiom.
